# Paper writing guidelines

See the [language guide](scientific_writing_guidelines_for_llms.md) for detailed scientific-writing instructions.

## Abbreviations

- Spell out every abbreviation in full at its first occurrence, followed by the abbreviation in parentheses, e.g., "electrocardiogram (ECG)". Use only the abbreviation after that.
- The abstract and the main text are separate: define an abbreviation again at its first occurrence in the main text, even if the abstract already defined it.
- Do not abbreviate a term that appears fewer than three times; write it out in full each time.
- Do not use abbreviations in the title or in section headings.

## Abstract

- Whether the abstract is structured (with headings) or unstructured (one paragraph), its sentences must cover these topics in this order:
  1. Introduction: the problem and the aim of the study.
  2. Methods: what was done and how.
  3. Results: the main findings, with numbers.
  4. Discussion: what the findings mean.
- Include at least one concrete quantitative result (e.g., effect size, accuracy, p-value).
- Do not use citations, references to figures or tables, or undefined abbreviations.

## Structure and argument

- The last paragraph of the Introduction states the aim or contribution of the paper.
- Every aim stated in the Introduction is explicitly answered in the Discussion or Conclusion.
- Each paragraph starts with a topic sentence that states its main point.
- Keep results and interpretation separate: the Results section reports, the Discussion interprets.
- The Discussion includes a paragraph on limitations.

## Language

- Use one term for one concept throughout the paper; do not alternate between synonyms.
- Prefer active voice ("We measured…") over passive voice.
- Use the past tense for what you did and found; use the present tense for general facts and for what figures show.
- Avoid vague words such as "very", "significant" (unless meaning statistically significant), and "novel".

## Figures, tables, and numbers

- Refer to every figure and table in the text, in the order they appear.
- Every caption is understandable without the main text: it defines its abbreviations and states the units.
- Legends and annotations must not obscure data; place them in dedicated space outside the plotting area when practical.
- Check figures at their final typeset size: all labels must fit inside their intended regions and remain legible.
- Give every number with its unit, and use the same number of decimal places for the same kind of quantity.
- Report uncertainty (standard deviation or confidence interval) with every estimate.
